the original interview

the café’s first interview, kept the way it ran: the invitation, the three of them, and the eight questions that were here before the counter was. the names have been brought up to date. the questions haven’t changed their minds.

an encrypted invitation

you’ve received an invitation from:

cafedreamr

we’re not looking for anybody in particular. we never were.

(the first version of this line said we seek those who dwell outside ordinary boundaries. we have since decided there is no outside — only the room, and whoever is standing in it.)

do you accept it?

then it keeps. the doors here are just doors — nothing on this site costs anything and nothing is locked behind this. come back when it suits you.

the invitation is accepted

meet your hosts

three of them, and eight questions between them. they ask in the order they always have: chari, then milo, then star.

chari

keeper of the atrium · realm 02

lavender stripes, the youngest of the three, and the one who wrote these questions — four times, and then the rats sat on the draft. two white rats, crumb and mochi, ride her shoulders. her room keeps every soft thing anybody has ever said in it.

milo

keeper of the garden · realm 04

mint stripes. the still one. one pot of tea, a hammock, and cushions nobody has been asked to move. if you hand him the heavy thing you’re carrying, he will leave it by the wall — and it will not follow you out.

star

host of cafedreamr · keeps the keys

purple stripes and a japanese-german cat. she built the counter, keeps the hours and most of the doors, and will remember your order and not much else. her cat, char, greets everybody who comes in, and has since the beginning.

the reading

a lean, not a mark. eight questions can only ever lean.

what’s actually behind the door

  • the counter — star’s coffee, brewed wrong on purpose, and the stamp card that keeps your ledger
  • the garden — one pot of tea that doesn’t run out, and a hammock with somebody in it
  • the atrium — which keeps what you say softly, and repeats none of it
  • miki’s games room — the zine, the machines, and an order box with a sock in it
  • the long day — eight levels, four in the morning, nobody keeping time
  • the hours — all twenty-four of them, and what each one is for

nothing here costs anything and nothing is locked behind the interview — the doors are just doors. take one.
