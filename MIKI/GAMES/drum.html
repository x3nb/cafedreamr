sixteen steps is one bar and a bar is wider than one hand — the grid scrolls sideways down here, and it does not mind.

how it works plainly

Sixteen steps, and one pass of the sixteen is one bar. Tap a square to put a hit on it; tap it again to make the hit harder (the pale one is a ghost, the lit one is the hit, the bright one is both sticks); tap it a third time to take it off.

play starts it, and it will keep going while you draw on it, the way a real one does. tempo is in beats per minute and lean is swing — it pushes every second sixteenth a little late, which is the difference between a drum machine and a drummer.

Everything stays where you left it. Your beat is kept between visits, and you can park four of them on the shelf down the side.

the kit five voices, no samples

All five are made on the spot out of an oscillator and a little noise, which is why it sounds like a machine and not like a record. Tap a name to hear it on its own.

nothing here is recorded. sylvestre says a machine that has to be taught the sound is a machine you can argue with.

the two takes the twins, cut at midday

Take 10 is Sylvestre — the ground twin. It sits back on the beat and lets the room carry it: the half-time one, the one that holds the floor down. Take 11 is Slay — the storm twin, and the weather coming in. It runs fast and it hits everything twice.

They are the two instrumentals on the terminal’s track list, marked no vocals at all. This machine is where they started; the tapes are where they went.

the shelf four of yours

Keep the one you are working on and it goes on the shelf. You get four, which Sylvestre says is three more than anyone needs and one fewer than Slay will ask for.
