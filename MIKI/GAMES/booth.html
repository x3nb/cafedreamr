take a number, he decides when it’s over

Bobby is miki’s — her alter ego, the sock on her hand, and the only one she trusts to do the hiring. He has a booth, a clipboard, a mug that says nothing, and a queue that has never once been empty. He asks. You answer. He reads it. At the end he prints you a card whether he’s keeping you or not, because the wall by the door is made of exactly that.

bobby does the interviews. i don’t sit in — he decides who’s next and what he asks and when it’s done, and i have never once changed his mind for him, which he brings up more often than he needs to. sit down, be honest, and don’t be boring. boring is the only thing he actually holds against people. — miki, on the back of the booth’s own forms

how it works plainly

Take a number. Sit. He asks six questions and you answer however you want — type it out, or tap one of the suggested things and change it until it’s true. He pulls the questions and the mood out of the box fresh each time, so no two sittings are the same one.

He really does read the answer. He counts the honest words and the hedged ones, he notices the machines and the names you bring up, and he has very strong feelings about people who talk at him in business voice. There is no right answer. There is only your answer, and what he does with it.

Then he prints the card. He keeps a copy; the copies are on the wall by the door.

who he is miki’s, not the café’s

Bobby is miki’s alter ego — a sock puppet with a taste for glitter bombs and a small anarchist streak. His ideas arrive fully formed, are usually a little out there, and have a way of working out anyway. He does miki’s hiring and miki’s interviews, never the café’s. Pearl went through him before she joined the studio, and she only got the job after she sat exactly where you’re sitting.

he is CC-011 in the dossier deck. the picture is a sock. he chose the picture.

the queue he decides who’s next
