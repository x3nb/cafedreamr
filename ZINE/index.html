MIKI'S ZINE

written, cut & stapled by miki · no ads, no filler, no apologies · printed badly on purpose

editor’s note

so this is issue two. issue one came back from the photocopier smelling like burnt toner and hans filed a formal complaint about “archival integrity.” fine. this one’s better anyway: a brand-new realm, a castle of opal that turned up in the back of it while we were stapling, the committee of rats who designed the whole thing and kept minutes, a recipe that only works at dusk, the only drawing of me and the twins that will ever exist, one page out of my own journal that somebody filed under j, and the verse hans chose for us, which is at the back where he can keep an eye on it. plus a lookbook that actually slaps, a pattern book of seven things none of us have made yet, and a sticker with your name on it — figuratively.

it’s yours if you want it. that’s the whole point of a zine.

— the rat

the feature

The atrium of whispered truths

chari’s new realm — or: how to furnish a home with two rats and a hunch

chari — youngest of the three kittens, star’s little sister, apprentice to literally everyone — finally got a realm of her own: the atrium of whispered truths. it’s a garden-home. a glass roof that lets the stars in, a warm brick oven, trailing plants in every corner, and a moodboard wall of pinned fabric swatches and torn-out magazine pages. it smells like rain on warm stone.

she didn’t plan it alone. nobody plans anything alone around here. she planned it with a committee of two: crumb and mochi, her white rats — the same small companions who ride her shoulders through the loud days and keep her steady when the anxiety and the lab-memories get close. turns out they’re also excellent interior designers.

if a rat naps on it, it stays.

the rat method

  1. lay everything on the floor. every cushion, every swatch, every lamp. the floor is the moodboard, and the moodboard is the floor.
  2. release the committee. crumb goes first — crumb is bold, crumb has opinions. mochi follows — mochi is the inspector, and mochi does not care about your feelings.
  3. the nap test. whichever swatch gets slept on, stays. a doze is the highest rating we have. two rats asleep at once is a landmark decision.
  4. the crumb test. if crumb leaves crumbs on it, it belongs in the kitchen. if it comes out clean, it’s furniture you’re allowed to love.
  5. the tiebreak. if they disagree, it waits by the window until they don’t. daylight is a fair referee and a better designer than most of us.

the garden & the pets

the garden is half borrowed from milo’s rows — a raised bed of mint the rats are not supposed to raid (they raid it), a herb wall she labels in her own handwriting, and a small courtyard that crumb and mochi inspect on a strict schedule — from chari’s shoulders, which is where they live. the pets list reads: crumb (bold), mochi (inspector), and a jar of tadpoles that hans insists, at length, are “not pets.”

that’s the whole trick of it: the realm is calm because it was decided by two animals who only ever want somewhere warm to sleep. chari says it’s the first room she’s had that doesn’t feel like it’s waiting for something bad. we could all use a committee like that.

the castle

The castle made of opal

chari asked, yeshua built it, and the committee of two did the interiors — in one afternoon, by sleeping on it

here’s the part the feature didn’t have room for: the atrium isn’t finished, and it isn’t going to be. it grew. past the herb wall, at the back of the garden room, under the glass, there’s a castle — small, chunky, two storeys and a tower, cut out of opal and lit from the inside. chari asked for it the way she asks for things — quietly, and once. yeshua built it. that’s what he does: he strung the whole café into a web because star asked him to, he gave miki her place when she graduated, and now there is a castle in the atrium.

he built it out of opal, which is the stone miki’s place is cut from — chari liked the look so much she asked for the whole of it in the same rock. it is the only castle in this family with no job. the twins’ is a studio and a lookout, hans has a kingdom with paperwork in it, and miki’s cavern has amps in it. chari’s castle is for being small in. from upstairs you can see the oven; from the floor you can see the sky.

yeshua built the castle. the rats decided where the door goes. (they were right.)

the building committee — the minutes, in full

recorded by nobody · ratified by nap

01the door. crumb: the garden side. mochi: the oven side. mochi napped on the oven side. carried: the oven side.

02a second tower. crumb: yes. mochi: no. it waited by the window until they agreed. carried, eventually.

03the drawbridge. crumb walked it twice, in both directions, slowly. approved with one revision: it stays down. nobody is laying siege to a castle in a glass room at night.

04the moat. crumb: yes. mochi: absolutely not. open. the hole is filled in. the moat is under review.

05the windows. warm, small, low — so the oven can be seen from upstairs and the sky from the floor. unanimous.

06the name. it is a castle, not a palace. the committee was very clear about this. unanimous.

07the rule. nothing unkind is said inside it. anything true that has to be said is whispered, and heard gently. unanimous, and permanently in force.

that last one is the whole castle, really — and it’s where the atrium gets its name, which is a lot of name to get from two rats writing on a floor between naps. in the late afternoon the light comes through the opal and turns the whole room the colour of the inside of a shell. chari says it’s the first building she’s had that was made for her instead of around her. turn up with something to eat and she’ll let you sit in it.

crumb leaves crumbs in the castle. this was motion 08. we don’t talk about motion 08.

the kitchen

Candy apples made of light

two recipes from the atrium, taken down at the counter and tested by people who should not have been allowed to test them

recipe no. 01

candy apples made of light

ingredients: two (2) apples, one (1) pan, sugar enough to be embarrassing, and light — the afternoon kind, off a glass roof. no other kind of light will take.

method: melt the sugar until it is the colour of the last ten minutes of a day. hold the apple on its stick in the middle of it. do not stir, do not rush, do not look away for a second — the light climbs into the sugar like it has been waiting for the invitation. lift it out glowing. set it down. it stays lit for about an hour.

warning: eat it before you finish admiring it. a candy apple made of light that has gone out is just an apple with regrets.

miki’s note: it does not photograph. i tried — twice, with two cameras nobody is supposed to know about. the drawing above was done before the second batch went out. this page is the only proof.

recipe no. 02 · the atrium version

the same apple, after dark

ingredients: everything above, plus the roof open and every lamp off.

method: the same, but you make it where the sky can see what you’re doing. the light that takes to the sugar then is colder, it flickers blue at the edge of the shell, and it tastes like a held breath. serve it to whoever is still awake with you.

note: chari makes this one. she does not measure the sugar and it works anyway, which is unfair, and we have agreed to stop arguing about it on the advice of the committee.

the ink plate

Three faces, no dignity

the only drawing of miki and the twins in existence, and the reason there will not be a second one

the twins do not pose. this is documented: hans has asked them twice for a portrait for the archive, and both times they sent back a drawing of hans. the whole family is like this, which is why the family album is mostly photographs of the backs of people leaving rooms.

so this is what we have — miki in the middle, a twin on each shoulder, nothing in frame but the faces, because the hands are a separate negotiation and the negotiation is still going. the running joke in this house is that slay and sylvestre have never once been photographed holding anything. the running joke is true.

the plate prints in two inks: the blue of those eyes, and miki’s yellow, which she refused to surrender to the press. everything else is ink and paper, the way a drawing is supposed to be. flip plates to colour at the top of the page if you want to see what the yellow cost us.

the archive

A page out of my journal

found in a drawer marked “journal — personal, do not file,” which is where you file a thing you are not filing

found in the archive

dear diary.

today i made sylvestre laugh so hard he fell off the drum stool, and slay laughed so hard he had to sit down on the floor, and i have been thinking about it for six hours straight. i’ve put it in the log. the machines and the portals are the watch shift, i know that. but i think that might be the job.

also: i fixed the camera in the west corridor. also: hello hi hans.

dear diary. today i —

this page was in the archive. it isn’t any more. it was in a drawer marked journal — personal, do not file, and the tear runs through the next day’s entry, halfway down a sentence, with nothing above it damaged — which is either a coincidence or a message, and in this family it is never a coincidence.

we’ve printed it the way it survived: the paper, the handwriting, the struck-out word, and the place where she stopped writing. the archive has apologised for the drawer, the drawer has been re-labelled, and miki is keeping the page.

if you find the other half, don’t file it. bring it to the counter. — ed.

the scriptures

The verse kept for this issue

hans keeps the scriptures. he chose this one, and he would like you to know that it took four days

one verse goes round the whole realm every day, and it is the same verse for everybody in it — it sits under your name on the cup star hands you, and the long day ends with a scroll of the passages you walked through to get there. a zine can’t do that. a zine is stapled once and then picked up on whatever day you happen to find it. so this issue carries the issue’s verse instead, the same one in every copy, which we are told is the point of paper.

hans chose it, and the only thing he would say about the choosing was: the mansions. everybody here has an address of their own — a garden, an archive, a lookout, a cavern with amps in it, and now a castle of opal with two rats on the committee — and every one of them still eats at the same counter. he has offered to explain the rest of it properly, at length, in the archive, with tea.

the day’s verse is on star’s cup · the passages are in the long day · ask hans and clear an afternoon.

the press

The shop prints to order and posts to your door — shirts, a notebook, a sheet of stickers, nothing sitting in a stockroom. It is the other way to keep the rack full.

Every issue of the zine is free, and always will be. Fifty copies is the smallest run the printer will do, and miki pays for the whole thing up front — then gives every copy away. If you want to keep the rack full, the same fund that holds the realm open pays the printer.
