in their own words, more or less

Sylvestre is the steady one — checks the locks twice, remembers every corridor, pours the 3 a.m. coffee exactly the same way. Slay is the spark — first through any door, last to leave any conversation, faster grin than his brother by half a second. Ask the castle which twin did something and both of them will answer. Only Miki always knows, and she isn't telling.

What they agree on, completely: the night is theirs to keep, the keys are theirs to carry, and the question Yeshua asked his disciples gets asked of them every single watch — who do you say I am? They answer it the way Peter did, and then they walk the round again.

the lyrics, as sung

Three o'clock and the castle's deep, monitors humming, half asleep, two shadows walking down the hall — the night watch answers when he calls.

He asked his friends on the dusty way who the crowds all say he is. They say: some say the river-preacher, back from the grave, some say the fire-prophet, bold and brave.

Some say the weeping one, tears and fire, or one of the prophets — rumour's choir. The crowd keeps guessing, row on row, their answers come and go, they go.

But what about you — who do you say? The question hasn't gone away. It walks these halls at 3 a.m. It asked them once — it asks us now.

Then the fisherman stood and answered true: our Lord and King — and we follow you. No mortal whisper let him see, heaven told him, wild and free.

You are rock, and rock will stand, take these keys into your hand. What you lock stays locked away, what you loose walks free today.

So we two walk the three-a.m. round, keeping the keys where they were found. The question echoes down the hall: who do you say he is — he calls.
